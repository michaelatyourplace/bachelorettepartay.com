Where Is Free Parking in Downtown Charleston, SC?

Free parking hours, Sundays, street meters, and the best ways to avoid expensive downtown parking.

Looking for free parking in downtown Charleston, SC? The easiest options are city parking meters on Sundays, official city holidays, and outside paid meter hours. There are also city-operated surface lots with free hours. Just be careful: not every downtown parking spot is free, and parking garages have separate fees.

Quick answer: City of Charleston street meters generally require payment Monday–Saturday, 9 AM–6 PM. They are free after 6 PM, before 9 AM, on Sundays, and on official city holidays, unless another posted restriction applies. Check the city's official meter rules before your visit.

1. Free Street Parking After 6 PM

If you are visiting downtown for dinner or an evening walk, city-operated metered spaces can be a good option. Meter payments stop at 6 PM Monday through Saturday and resume at 9 AM on the next chargeable day. Only park in a legal space and read signs for loading zones, street sweeping, temporary closures, or other restrictions.

Important King Street exception: Street parking on King Street between Spring and John streets is prohibited from 6 PM to 3 AM Thursday, Friday, and Saturday. A free meter period does not override that restriction.

2. Free Downtown Parking on Sundays

Sunday is one of the best times to visit without paying for a city street meter. City-operated meters are not charged on Sundays. You might find spaces around the Historic District, King Street side streets, and other metered blocks, but availability is never guaranteed. Residential permit signs and other parking restrictions still apply.

Official city holidays are also exempt from street meter charges. See the City of Charleston parking guide.

3. City Surface Lots With Free Hours

Charleston's city-operated metered surface lots generally charge from 9 AM to 6 PM Monday through Saturday and are free during the non-charging hours, including Sundays and official city holidays. These are not the same as parking garages.

4. Is Parking Free Near King Street or the City Market?

Sometimes. If you find a legal city metered street space, the same free evening and Sunday rules apply. But blocks near King Street and the Charleston City Market can be busy. Do not assume a privately operated lot or garage is free just because meters are not charging.

If you're visiting for nightlife, remember the special Thursday–Saturday night restriction on the section of King Street from Spring to John streets.

5. Free Parking for Eligible Disability Placards

Visitors with a qualifying state-issued disability placard or plate may park without charge in eligible publicly operated parking meters, lots, and garages under the applicable rules. Private facilities may still charge. Read the city's disability parking guidance for details.

6. If You Can't Find Free Parking

Downtown city garages are usually paid, even on Sundays. If street spaces are full, compare the official garage list and rates. Some city garages offer discounted evening parking, but those discounts are not free parking.

You can also leave your car in a legal paid space and ride the free CARTA DASH downtown shuttle to explore more of the peninsula. The shuttle is free; parking where you board it might not be. See CARTA routes and schedules and Explore Charleston's parking tips.

Free Downtown Charleston Parking: Frequently Asked Questions

Is downtown Charleston parking free on Sunday?

Yes, city-operated metered street spaces and city metered surface lots do not charge on Sundays. Private parking facilities and city garages may still charge.

Is street parking free after 6 PM?

City street meters stop charging at 6 PM, but posted restrictions still apply. Pay special attention to the Thursday–Saturday King Street evening prohibition.

Can I park overnight for free?

Some legal street spaces do not require meter payment overnight, but they may have time limits, permit restrictions, or other signs. Check carefully before leaving a car overnight; never assume all-night parking is allowed.
